## The 16 edges not found in label text, and why

A keyword search only finds the words a label uses. None of these 16 edges was removed; each is explained here so a reader can judge it.

- **Diuretics → urinary incontinence** (furosemide, torsemide, hydrochlorothiazide). The labels describe increased and urgent urination. In an older adult, that is what leads to incontinence, and the pattern is documented in the cascade literature.
- **Anticholinergic effects of older antihistamines** (meclizine: cognitive impairment, urinary retention, constipation; dimenhydrinate: cognitive impairment, urinary retention; hydroxyzine: urinary retention). Their labels mostly say drowsiness and dry mouth. These harms in older adults come from the AGS Beers criteria.
- **Older-adult harms worded differently** (lorazepam → falls, a Beers criterion for benzodiazepines; galantamine → urinary incontinence, a known cholinesterase-inhibitor cascade; omeprazole → vitamin B12 deficiency and fracture, warnings on prescription PPI labels, so the label matched here may be the over-the-counter one).
- **Labels with no adverse-reactions section** (senna → diarrhoea, loperamide → constipation, calcium + vitamin D → constipation). Over-the-counter Drug Facts labels and supplements have no such section to search.
